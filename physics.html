Engineering a Safer School Backpack System

Discover how work and power, energy, momentum, and rotational motion explain the mechanics of lifting, carrying, and managing a school backpack.

Explore the concepts ↓

THE BIG IDEA

A lighter bag means less mechanical load.

Students may carry textbooks, notebooks, water bottles, devices, and other items that are not needed for every class. The report proposes reducing unnecessary backpack mass through timetable-based packing, lockers or shared storage, and proper backpack fit.

Important: Physics equations explain mechanical demands. By themselves, they do not prove that a particular backpack causes or prevents pain or injury.

FOUR CONNECTED IDEAS

The physics behind your backpack

Four physics topics explain different parts of lifting and carrying a loaded bag.

↥

01 · LIFTING

Work and Power

Work is done against gravity when a backpack is lifted. Power describes how quickly that work is done.

W = mgh
P = W / t

Where: m = mass, g = gravitational acceleration, h = lift height, and t = time.

◉

02 · MOVEMENT

Energy and Work–Energy

A raised backpack gains gravitational potential energy. A moving backpack has kinetic energy.

ΔU = mgΔh
KE = ½mv²

The work–energy theorem connects net work with the change in kinetic energy: Wnet = ΔKE.

⇢

03 · STOPPING

Momentum and Collisions

Momentum depends on mass and velocity. Average force depends on how quickly momentum changes.

p = mv
Favg = Δp / Δt

For the same momentum change, a longer stopping time means a smaller average force.

⤺

04 · BALANCE

Rotational Motion and Torque

Torque describes the turning effect of a force around a pivot, such as the body when a backpack is carried.

τ = rF sin θ
Fg = mg

Keeping a backpack closer to the back reduces the moment arm r, which can reduce torque in the simplified model.

PUBLISHED STUDY EXAMPLE

What changed when the bag got lighter?

The supplied report describes a 2022 school-based educational program in which the experimental group's average backpack mass changed from 3.18 kg to 2.08 kg. The program included several behaviors, so the result cannot be attributed to lockers alone.

Before3.18 kg
After2.08 kg
34.6%reported mean mass reduction

Source cited in the report: NeJhaddadgar et al. (2022), Journal of Primary Care & Community Health.

A WORKED EXAMPLE

Compare the work needed to lift a backpack

The report uses a vertical lift height of 0.75 m and gravitational acceleration of 9.81 m/s².

Before the intervention

3.18 kg

W = mgh

W = (3.18)(9.81)(0.75)
≈ 23.40 J
→
After the intervention

2.08 kg

W = mgh

W = (2.08)(9.81)(0.75)
≈ 15.30 J

Difference: about 8.10 J less mechanical work for this single lift under the stated assumptions.

THE PROPOSED SYSTEM

Four practical ways to reduce unnecessary load

01

Locker access

Store textbooks and materials that are not needed for upcoming periods.

02

Timetable-based packing

Check the daily class schedule and bring only the materials required.

03

Shared classroom storage

Use supervised shelves or racks when individual lockers are unavailable.

04

Proper backpack fit

Use both shoulder straps, adjust them appropriately, and keep heavier items close to the back.

HOW TO TEST IT

Measure first. Compare later.

The report does not include original measurements from La Salle Academy. A future school evaluation could compare backpack mass before and after the system is introduced, under consistent conditions.

  • Record baseline backpack mass with a calibrated scale.
  • Implement the storage and packing strategies over a defined trial period.
  • Measure backpack mass again under the same conditions.
  • Compare results and document limitations instead of assuming outcomes.
